Why grocery checkout needs to be fast

A grocery or general trade shop doesn't usually deal in a handful of high-value sales a day — it deals in many small ones, often with a queue forming behind each customer. Checkout speed isn't a nice-to-have here; it's the difference between a line that moves and one that doesn't. Kaynko POS's checkout is built to complete a sale — scan, total, payment — in under 10 seconds.

Barcode scanning at speed

Scan packaged goods with the phone's camera, or pair a Bluetooth barcode scanner for higher daily volume. Either way, items go straight into the cart without typing a product name.

Low-stock alerts on fast movers

Grocery items sell out fast. Low-stock alerts flag items running low so a reorder happens before a shelf goes empty, not after.

Every payment method a grocery customer uses

Cash, mobile money, card, bank transfer, and credit for regular customers — all recorded the same way, with Paynow available to confirm card and mobile money payments in Zimbabwe.

Works through a power cut or a dropped connection

Checkout doesn't pause for a lost connection. All sales and stock data live on the phone itself and sync automatically once connectivity returns.

Stock accuracy matters more with high turnover

With hundreds of small items moving daily, a stock count that drifts from reality compounds fast. Every sale deducts from the recorded stock level automatically, and receiving new stock — with its cost per unit — updates both the quantity on hand and the shop's cost records in the same step, so a reorder is based on what's actually there.

Running credit for regular customers

Many general trade shops extend informal credit to regular customers — a running tab paid off periodically. Kaynko POS tracks each customer's balance, supports a credit limit if you want one, and records partial payments against it, replacing a notebook with something that updates itself and can be backed up.
