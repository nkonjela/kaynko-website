Bluetooth Thermal Printer Setup for Android POS Apps: A Step-by-Step Guide

Kaynko POS Team · 6 min read

A Bluetooth thermal printer is the most common piece of extra hardware a shop adds to a phone-based POS setup. It's inexpensive, needs no ink or toner, and pairs with the phone the same way a Bluetooth speaker or headset would. Here's how to get one working, and what to check if it isn't.

What you need

  • A Bluetooth thermal receipt printer (58mm or 80mm paper width, depending on the model).
  • Thermal paper for that printer.
  • An Android phone with Bluetooth and location permissions enabled (Android requires location permission for Bluetooth device scanning — this is an Android platform requirement, not specific to any one app).

Pairing the printer

  1. Turn the printer on and make sure it has paper loaded.
  2. Put the printer into pairing mode (usually a button press — check the printer's own manual, since this varies by model).
  3. In Kaynko POS, go to Settings → Printer and tap to scan for nearby devices.
  4. Select your printer from the list and confirm the pairing.
  5. Print a test receipt to confirm the connection before relying on it for a real sale.

Once paired, the app remembers the printer and reconnects to it automatically the next time you open it — there's no need to re-pair every session.

If it won't connect

Most Bluetooth printer problems come down to one of a few things:

  • Bluetooth or location permission not granted. Check the app's permissions in Android's system settings if the scan finds nothing.
  • The printer isn't actually in pairing mode. Many printers need the pairing button held, not just tapped, or need to be freshly powered on.
  • The printer is already paired to another phone's Bluetooth settings. Forget the device in the other phone's system Bluetooth menu first.
  • Low battery. Some printers behave unreliably, or won't pair at all, when their battery is nearly empty.

If it connects but prints garbled text

This is almost always a paper-width mismatch — a 58mm printer fed with 80mm-formatted output (or vice versa) will produce cut-off or stretched text. Confirm the printer's actual paper width against what's loaded, and check the app's receipt settings match it.

Why Bluetooth instead of a cable or Wi-Fi printer

Bluetooth printers need no cables running across a counter and no shared Wi-Fi network to be reachable — the phone talks directly to the printer. That matters especially for a shop that can't always count on a stable internet connection: printing a receipt never depends on the internet being up, only on the printer being paired and in range.
