Multi-Cashier PIN Logins: Why Shared Devices Need Per-Staff Accountability

Kaynko POS Team · 6 min read

As soon as a shop has more than one person ringing up sales, a single shared login stops being harmless convenience and starts being a real gap. If every sale, void, and return is logged under "the shop account" rather than a specific person, there's no way to answer a simple question: who actually did this?

The problem with one shared login

A shared login works fine for a one-person shop. The moment a second or third staff member starts using the same device, a few things quietly become impossible to track: which cashier was on shift when a variance showed up at the end of the day, which cashier voided a sale, and which cashier processed a particular return. None of that is a sign anyone did anything wrong — it's just that there's no record to check either way.

How PIN-based accounts fix it

Kaynko POS gives each staff member their own short PIN. When a cashier opens the app, they log in with their own PIN, and every sale, void, return, and cash movement they make is tied to their account specifically. At the end of the day, a shop owner or manager can see exactly who sold what, who opened and closed which cash session, and where any cash variance actually occurred.

This isn't about surveillance — it's the same basic accountability a till with individual staff cards or codes has always provided, just without needing separate hardware per cashier.

Manager approval on sensitive actions

Beyond just logging who did what, certain actions — voiding a sale, processing a return, adjusting stock, reversing a debt payment — can require a manager's PIN to approve, separate from the cashier performing the action. That creates a second layer: not just a record of who did something, but confirmation that someone with manager-level trust signed off on it. Both the approver and the person who carried out the action are recorded, not just one or the other.

What this looks like day to day

  • A cashier starts their shift by logging in with their own PIN, not a shared one.
  • They open a cash session, count their float, and start selling — under their own name.
  • If they need to void a sale past a certain time window, or process a return, a manager approves it with their own PIN.
  • At close of day, the end-of-day report shows exactly whose session it was, and the manager dashboard shows every cashier's activity for the period.

Why this matters even for a small team

It's tempting to think staff accountability is only worth setting up once a shop has a large team. In practice, the smaller the team, the more a single unexplained variance stands out — and the more useful it is to be able to say definitively whose shift it happened on, rather than guessing. Setting it up from the start costs nothing extra; it's a setting, not a separate purchase.
